Pasado continuo: I was doing

Unidad 13

Sirve para decir que algo estaba pasando en un momento del pasado: había empezado antes y aún no había terminado. En español: "estaba trabajando", "estaba lloviendo".

was / were + verbo-ing

AfirmativaNegativaPregunta
I / he / she / itI was working.I wasn't working.Was she working?
we / you / theyThey were working.They weren't working.Were you working?

Es igual que el presente continuo, pero cambiando am / is / are por was / were:

  • I'm working now. → I was working at 10 o'clock last night.
  • What are you doing? → What were you doing at 3 o'clock?
  • At 9.30 Lucía was unpacking boxes. (empezó a las 9 y terminó a las 10)
  • Today she's wearing a skirt, but yesterday she was wearing jeans.
  • Sorry, I wasn't listening.

Pistas

at 5 o'clock yesterday (ayer a las 5) · at 10 last night · this time last year (el año pasado por estas fechas) · while (mientras).

Cómo se escribe el -ing

  • Casi siempre se añade -ing: work → working, fold → folding.
  • Si acaba en -e, se quita la e: serve → serving, have → having.
  • Si acaba en vocal + consonante (corto), se dobla: put → putting, run → running, sit → sitting.

La clave: una acción larga cortada por otra corta

Cuando se juntan las dos, la acción larga (la que ya estaba pasando) va en pasado continuo y la corta (la que la interrumpe) en pasado simple.

larga: was / were + -ing · corta: pasado simple

  • I was serving a customer when the phone rang. → estaba atendiendo (larga) cuando sonó (corta).
  • Ana fell off the ladder while she was putting jeans on the top shelf.
  • It was raining when I left work. → ya llovía antes de salir.
  • When (cuando) suele ir con la acción corta; while (mientras) con la larga.

Ojo: cambia el significado

  • When I arrived, Marta was opening the boxes. → ya estaba abriéndolas cuando llegué.
  • When I arrived, Marta opened the boxes. → llegué y entonces las abrió.

Dos acciones cortas seguidas → las dos en pasado simple

  • The customer paid and left.
  • When the phone was ringing, I answered it. → When the phone rang, I answered it.

Present perfect: I have done

Unidad 15

Sirve para algo que pasó antes y tiene un resultado ahora. En español suele ser "he hecho, has perdido, se ha ido".

have / has + participio

AfirmativaNegativaPregunta
I / we / you / theyI have ('ve) lost it.I haven't lost it.Have you lost it?
he / she / itShe has ('s) gone.She hasn't gone.Has she gone?
  • I've lost my keys. → ahora no las tengo.
  • Where's Ana? — She's gone home. → ahora no está.
  • We've sold all the black T-shirts. → ahora no quedan.
  • Somebody has broken the mirror. → ahora está roto.

El participio (la 3.ª columna)

En los verbos regulares es igual que el pasado, con -ed: clean → cleaned, close → closed, tidy → tidied. Los irregulares hay que aprenderlos:

  • She has went home. → She has gone home. (con has/have nunca va el pasado)
  • She have gone. → She has gone. (he / she / it → has)
  • She's gone = she has gone. El 's puede ser is o has: si detrás va un participio, es has.

Ojo: nunca con una fecha terminada

Si dices cuándo pasó (yesterday, last week, at 5 o'clock), usa el pasado simple:

  • I have seen Ana yesterday. → I saw Ana yesterday.

just, already y yet

Unidad 16
PalabraSignificaDónde vaEjemplo
justhace un momento ("acabar de")entre have/has y el participioShe's just gone home.
alreadyya (antes de lo esperado)entre have/has y el participioWe've already sold all the scarves.
yettodavía no (negativa) · ya (pregunta)al final de la fraseThe delivery hasn't arrived yet.
  • Just: Are you hungry? — No, I've just had lunch. (acabo de comer)
  • Already: Don't forget to pay. — I've already paid. (ya he pagado)
  • Yet en negativa: I haven't had lunch yet. (todavía no he comido)
  • Yet en pregunta: Have you finished yet? — No, not yet. (¿ya has terminado? — Todavía no)

Truco

  • Frase afirmativa → just o already. Negativa o pregunta → yet.
  • I haven't had lunch already. → I haven't had lunch yet.
  • She's yet gone. → She's just gone.
